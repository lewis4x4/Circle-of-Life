# Facility operations source links — COL-147

Status: PARTIAL — HFO-09 shared mechanism. September 10, 2026. Extends the [occurrences](27-facility-operations-occurrences.md), [receipts](27-facility-operations-receipts.md) and [corrections](27-facility-operations-corrections.md) contracts under BUILD-SCOPE section 4 ("Execution receipt … source record/version"), section 5 ("Link source event") and section 8 ("Every adapter has a tested matching predicate and failure behavior. Source record existence alone is insufficient"). Source implementation only: it registers no adapter, reads no real domain table, declares nothing final and establishes no hosted, provider, staff or operating acceptance. The domain connections are COL-154 through COL-159.

## A source record satisfies an occurrence only through an allowlisted reader, read live

An adapter is an allowlist row (`operation_source_adapters`) naming a `source_key`, the typed subject kind it describes and a reader function `haven.<name>(text) RETURNS jsonb` that returns the live state of one record: whether it exists, its version, its finality (`draft`, `final`, `voided`), its site, the activity it satisfies, its typed subject, its author and recording instant, and a statement of the work in the exact shape a person's recording carries. A second allowlist (`operation_source_rules`) names the activities a source may satisfy. Both are written only by migration; no session or service role can register, retire or alter them, and migration 346 registers none.

Every delivery re-reads the source through its reader at that moment and trusts nothing the caller claims beyond the record's identity, version and the site it belongs to. A session may deliver only for a site it currently holds and, for a protected subject kind, only with that domain's recording scope, checked before the reader runs; a reply or a converged ledger row is returned only to someone who may read it. A ledger row keeps the reader's full answer only when a subject row was resolved; otherwise it keeps identity and finality alone, and a site mismatch keeps nothing but the refusal. A record that does not exist, is a draft, is voided when a final is delivered (or final when a void is delivered), is now a different version, belongs to another site, names a non-allowlisted activity, names a subject of the wrong kind, has no enrolled subject at the site, whose author is unknown to the organisation or outside the published recorder list, whose statement fails the 341 rules, or whose performed instant lies in the future, cannot satisfy anything. Each such delivery is a recorded row in the ledger with its machine reason and the refusing wording, never a silent drop and never a receipt.

## The matching predicate and one satisfaction

The predicate is activity (from the reader, allowlisted), site (claimed and returned, both must agree), subject (the reader's native id resolved to the one `operation_activity_subjects` row for that site), period (the managed `scheduled` or `event` occurrence whose period contains the performed instant's local date in the occurrence's own timezone; cancelled and removed rows are never candidates) and rule (the occurrence's pinned requirement version and site configuration, against which the statement is validated exactly as a human recording is, with the late rule anchored on the source's own recording instant).

Exactly one candidate with no effective performance receipt is satisfied: the mechanism writes a 341 performance receipt whose recorder is the source author, whose recorded-at is the server clock at delivery, and which carries `source_event_id`, `source_key`, `source_record_id` and `source_record_version`; the occurrence projection, evidence evaluation, review requirement and audit are the 341/344 behaviour. Missing required evidence leaves the occurrence performed with missing evidence; a review-required rule leaves it awaiting verification. A candidate that already carries a person's receipt, or another source's, is a `conflict` that overwrites nothing. No candidate is `unmatched`; several are `ambiguous`. A second delivery of the same version and kind, under the same or another request key, by a session or the service, converges on the one live ledger row and returns it as a replay; a refused delivery is history that never blocks a later one. Concurrent deliveries serialise on a per-record advisory lock and on the occurrence row. A record is the effective receipt of at most one occurrence at a time (a partial unique index enforces it): a later version whose performed date falls in another period first invalidates the earlier receipt on the first occurrence, with a reason naming both versions and the move, and then satisfies the second as a fresh chain.

## Correction and void append history

A later final version of a record that already satisfied an occurrence is a 344 correction: the new receipt names the earlier source receipt, extends its chain, states the reason (source, record, both versions), carries finalized evidence across the chain and supersedes a bound review so the occurrence visibly awaits review again when the rule requires it. The corrected receipt stays verbatim. A version whose receipt has since been corrected by a person is a `conflict`, not an overwrite.

A `voided` delivery matches by source identity, not by period. When the effective performance receipt is the one this source wrote, a 344 reversal supersedes it and its review, returns the occurrence to `pending` or `missed` by its deadline, and the ledger row stays `invalidated` with attention until an administrator dismisses it with a reason. When the source's receipt was already corrected by a person or reversed, the void is refused as `source_not_effective` with attention: the person's restatement is not erased by the void. A void of a record that never satisfied anything is refused as `nothing_to_invalidate`.

## Pending reconciliation is visible and bounded

Rows in `unmatched`, `ambiguous`, `conflict` or `invalidated`, refusals for an author outside the recorder list or no longer active, and transient reader failures, carry `attention` and are readable under current site, occurrence and subject authority. `reconcile_operation_source_event_review` requires the COL-133 broad operations scope at the site, the expected event revision (a moved revision is a conflict naming the current one) and a request key; every attempt is an immutable row. `retry` re-reads the live source and re-runs the predicate (a reader that cannot answer leaves the row untouched); `select` chooses one of the predicate's current candidates and nothing else; `dismiss` needs a reason and touches no occurrence. The delivery's own snapshot never changes; each attempt keeps what it read. Reconciliation never overrides the period.

## Boundaries

No adapter, reader, rule, source, scheduler, reminder, notification or external transmission. The service wrapper exists for later outbox drains and is executable only by the service role; the session wrapper refuses the service identity and the service wrapper refuses a session. The migration slot 346 is provisional on this stack (Finance-first renumbering at integration). Engineering policies awaiting the owner are listed in `docs/facility-operations/OWNER-DECISIONS.md` (3j).

## API

- `POST /api/admin/operations/source-events` — `{ request_key, payload: { source_key, source_record_id, source_record_version, event_kind, facility_id } }` → `{ outcome: "receipt", event, occurrence | null, receipt | null, candidates, replayed }`; bounded outcome classes as the receipt routes.
- `GET /api/admin/operations/source-events?facility_id=&attention=true|false&state=` — the site ledger read through the session, paged to exhaustion with the exact total.
- `POST /api/admin/operations/source-events/[id]/reconcile` — `{ request_key, expected_revision, payload: { action: retry | select | dismiss, occurrence_id?, reason? } }`; a moved revision returns `409` with `current_event_revision`.
