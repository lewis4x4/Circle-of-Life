3c. **Occurrence engineering policies to confirm or change (COL-139).** Six deterministic choices are tested and reported, not hidden; each is a small change if preferred. (i) A facility-kind activity needs no explicit binding: the applicable, confirmed site configuration enrols the site itself, and the generator resolves or creates the site's one facility subject; residents, employees and assets are generated only from an explicit effective-dated binding made by a site administrator who holds that subject's authority. (iii) Managed occurrences refuse the legacy defer and reinstate commands because those create replacement rows without identity; a scoped defer for managed rows is not designed here. (iv) Manual unscheduled work requires a central version in force and a site configuration in force whose applicability is not `not_applicable`; a `needs_confirmation` schedule is expected. (v) An association of early, late or unknown-schedule work to a scheduled occurrence is one-to-one and records nothing about completion; whether an association satisfies the occurrence is COL-142's decision. (vi) A binding may name a shift and the generation command accepts shifted evaluator items, but the version-1 rule shape carries no shift, so the scheduler never sends one and a shifted binding reports `no_binding`; shift-scoped generation waits for a shifted rule shape rather than inventing a shift from the deadline time. Evidence: `col139-evidence/`.
3d. **Receipt engineering policies to confirm or change (COL-142).** Seven deterministic choices are tested and reported, not hidden; each is a small change if preferred. (i) A performed time later than the server clock plus two minutes is refused; a performed time earlier than fifteen minutes before recording requires an explicit `late` entry with a reason. (ii) Until COL-143 supplies verified evidence, every required evidence rule that applies to the outcome is unmet, so an evidence-requiring activity records as `performed_missing_evidence` and its occurrence stays in progress; nothing reports it complete. (iii) Outcomes are `performed`, `failed` (an issue is created with the receipt) and `not_performed` (a reason is required); Q10's question of what allows an item to count as done is not answered by these values. (iv) Only `completed` changes the occurrence status; `awaiting_verification`, `performed_missing_evidence`, `failed` and `not_performed` keep it `in_progress` with the state visible, rather than adding new status values to the legacy check. (v) `completed_at` is the server instant of the receipt that completed the occurrence and `performed_at` is the stated work instant; `sla_met` compares the performed instant with the grace end or deadline. (vi) An occurrence that carries recorded work cannot be cancelled and a cancelled occurrence cannot be verified; changing recorded work is a correction (HFO-08). (vii) For managed occurrences the published recorder list (the site's local list when set, else the central list) decides who may record; the legacy queue hint `assigned_role` no longer gates recording on managed rows, because the order of a published role list is not a rule anyone wrote down. Evidence: `col142-evidence/`.
3e. **Issue lifecycle engineering policies to confirm or change (COL-144).** Seven deterministic choices are tested and reported, not hidden; each is a small change if preferred. (i) Assigning, reassigning and reopening an issue is a manager's act (the COL-133 broad operations list); the reporter may also reopen; the owner and backup may wait, resume and resolve. (ii) A backup may take over only when the owner is no longer current at the site (inactive, deleted, grant revoked or expired) or with an explicit cover reason; the takeover is recorded as `covered`, never as the owner's own acceptance. (iii) Waiting requires a reason and a future follow-up instant; the backlog flags an overdue follow-up but nothing is notified (Q29 open; HFO-15 owns reminders). (iv) Reopening clears the resolution columns on the row and keeps every resolution verbatim in the event history; the reopen count is visible. (v) Ownership is never inferred from a role name or a duty split (Q02 open): an issue is unassigned until someone names an owner, and the backlog shows it as such. (vi) An occurrence that still carries an unresolved issue cannot be cancelled or removed, so open work never leaves the backlog through a cancellation; an issue whose native subject stops being current (asset retired, resident transferred, employee terminated) hides with the subject under the COL-133 rule and is not resolvable until a historical-record scope exists, which is the COL-133 boundary rather than a new choice; likewise a resolved issue whose occurrence is later removed becomes unreadable with the occurrence while its rows and events stay in the tables. (vii) Owners and backups must hold an operations role (the list site access enforces); a person without one can be neither, even with a site grant. Evidence: `col144-evidence/`.
3f. **Evidence engineering policies to confirm or change (COL-143, source portion).** Five deterministic choices are tested and reported, not hidden; each is a small change if preferred. (i) One private bucket `operation-evidence` with four allowed types (PDF, JPEG, PNG, WebP) and a twenty-mebibyte limit; the evidence id owns the object path. (ii) Checksum verification is in source (amendment `col143-evidence/checksum-contracts.md`, 2026-09-10): the client declares the MD5 of the bytes it uploads and the database verifies it against the Storage service's eTag, the one content fingerprint the client did not write, when the upload is marked and again at finalization; a mismatch or a changed object fails the row durably (`checksum_mismatch` / `object_changed`, no exception, both values on the event), and an eTag that is not a plain MD5 leaves the row unverified and it never finalizes. An optional SHA-256 stays informational. The hosted proof gains one confirmation step (COL-143-HANDOFF, step 4): confirm the hosted eTag of a signed-URL upload is the plain MD5 of the bytes and that `owner` is the uploader; if it is not, nothing finalizes until the recorded fallback (an app-tier verifier streaming the object through the session's signed URL under a database-held secret) is proven. (iii) Only the uploader may mark, finalize or fail an in-flight row, and only the uploader can see it before it is finalized; an abandoned in-flight row attaches to nothing and blocks nothing, and a fresh preparation by any recorder proceeds. Supplementary evidence (no rule label) never counts toward satisfaction and never blocks it. (iv) When later evidence completes a performance, the occurrence completes at the finalization instant with the original performed-at unchanged; the finalizer is recorded on the evidence event, and the receipt's completion mirrors are written as the recorder's own completion (no second performer is invented). (v) `reading` evidence rules stay unmet until a later issue satisfies them from receipt values. Evidence: `col143-evidence/`.
3g. **Correction engineering policies to confirm or change (COL-145).** Seven deterministic choices are tested and reported, not hidden; each is a small change if preferred. (iv) Finalized evidence carries across a correction chain and never across a reversal; an upload in flight during a correction stays on the superseded receipt and must be re-prepared. (v) A correction always reopens review when the rule requires review, superseding the earlier review; independence is judged against the effective receipt. (vi) A reversal returns the occurrence to `pending` or `missed` by its deadline; cancellation after a reversal is allowed; an issue created with a receipt that is later superseded or reversed stays open and bound to that receipt. (vii) Legacy writers (complete, defer, reinstate, escalate, bulk, meeting synchronisation, service or session updates) refuse managed rows rather than being translated into receipts; the ordinary start transition and the scheduler's legacy-only insert are retained as COL-142 and COL-139 decided.
